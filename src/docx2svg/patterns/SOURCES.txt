Hyphenation patterns and word lists used by docx2svg.hyphenate
==============================================================

Every file here but hyph-en-us-knuth.*, en-moby.txt.gz and en-observed-exceptions.txt is
an unmodified copy of a file of the hyph-utf8
package, as published in the tex-hyphen repository:

    https://github.com/hyphenation/tex-hyphen
    commit 5684c0f51c0b81133db2efbe60a408b4155a3ff5
    directory hyph-utf8/tex/generic/hyph-utf8/patterns/txt/

The .pat.txt files hold the patterns, one per line; the .hyp.txt files hold the
exceptions, one word per line with its hyphens.  Each language's licence, as its
hyph-utf8 source (patterns/tex/hyph-<tag>.tex) states it, is below.  Every one permits
copying, modification and redistribution in an MIT-licensed project; the copyright
notices and licence texts are kept here as they require.

hyph-en-us-knuth.* are converted, in the same format, from two files on CTAN (below).
en-moby.txt.gz is converted from the Moby Hyphenator list (below).

None of this data comes from Microsoft Word or its proofing tools.  Word's own break
points are observed by tools/make_hyphen_probe.py and kept in the probe's recording
(tests/fixtures/hyphen-observations.json), where they score these patterns; the one file
made from them is en-observed-exceptions.txt (below).

Languages whose patterns are not here are not hyphenated by the library.


en-observed-exceptions.txt -- English, the probe's own words (consulted before en-moby)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Behavioural observations of Word's output, not Microsoft data: no file of Word or its
  proofing tools was read.  The words are those of the repository's own probe documents
  (tools/make_hyphen_probe.py, the points and words sweeps, made to score the patterns);
  each is spelt with the points at which Word 16.106 was seen breaking it, read off
  Word's PDF of the sweep, as recorded in tests/fixtures/hyphen-observations.json
  (recorded 2026-10-01 and 2026-10-02; this file made 2026-10-03 by tools/read_hyphen_probe.py
  --exceptions, from the recording alone, with no Word run).

  It holds the 42 of the 359 English probe words whose points without compatibility mode
  or in mode 15 differ from those en-moby.txt.gz and the patterns give, one a line, "="
  at each point; after a space, the word as Word breaks it in mode 15 where that is not
  the same points less those within three letters of the end (8 words).  No word holds a
  string this repository keeps out of its files (none was left out).  The 359 words score
  every English set (ROADMAP.md, 3.9.1); with this file they all agree, which says
  nothing of other words -- the score without it does.


en-moby.txt.gz -- English word list (every en-* tag; consulted before the patterns)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The Moby Hyphenator II list, by Grady Ward, as Project Gutenberg distributes it (etext
  3204):

    https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3204/files/mhyph.txt
    fetched 2026-10-03, 2,602,250 bytes,
    SHA-256 eeb30474c86b8af3469035ec1a0913e35905325ca885290db9dfed9881e230ac

  licence: public domain.  The distribution's notes (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3204/
  3204-0.txt) read: "This documentation, the software and/or database are: Public Domain
  material by grant from the author, January, 2001."  Project Gutenberg's catalogue entry
  (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3204) gives its copyright status as "Public domain in
  the USA."  No notice needs keeping; this one is kept for the record.

  The file as fetched: 187,175 entries, one a line, CRLF; Mac Roman (0x8E is e-acute...);
  each break point marked by byte 0xA5 (U+2022 BULLET in Mac Roman), which the notes call
  "ASCII 165"; a word with no mark is one that should not be broken.  Converted thus:

  1. decoded as Mac Roman, split at CRLF;
  2. left out: 4 entries that hold a string this repository keeps out of its files (as for
     ushyphex.tex below);
  3. 166,299 entries are a single word (letters and marks only) and are kept: a mark at
     either end is dropped (one entry), and of a spelling listed more than once (1,988
     spellings) the later entry is kept;
  4. the other 20,872 entries -- phrases, compounds with a hyphen, words with an
     apostrophe, abbreviations with a slash or a digit -- are not kept as entries, since
     the library hyphenates the parts of a compound and the letters between apostrophes as
     words of their own; a part (letters and marks only) whose case-folded spelling no
     single-word entry has is kept as a word (3,515 words, the later of its listings);
  5. the mark written as "=" (not a hyphen, which a spelling may hold), the 167,826 words
     sorted by their case-folded spelling, one a line, UTF-8, and gzip-compressed (level 9,
     no name, mtime 0): 593,404 bytes, SHA-256 7451adf4b6ccb91a...  The decompressed text
     is never kept in the repository.

  Lookup (hyphenate.WordList): by the word's case-folded spelling; the spelling as typed
  if listed (Polish, polish), else the lower-case one, else the first.  Of the open English
  sources measured it agrees with Word's breaks most often (ROADMAP.md, 3.9.1); the
  patterns below break only the words it does not hold.


hyph-en-us-knuth.pat.txt, hyph-en-us-knuth.hyp.txt -- English (used for every en-* tag)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The patterns are those of the Plain TeX hyphenation table, hyphen.tex (Donald E. Knuth
  and Frank M. Liang; CTAN macros/plain/base/hyphen.tex, SHA-256 2c18acdc04c1a066...),
  one per line.  The exceptions are hyphen.tex's own fourteen words and then those of
  "Hyphenation exceptions for US English, based on hyphenation exception log articles in
  TUGboat" (CTAN info/digests/tugboat/hyphenex/ushyphex.tex, 2021, SHA-256
  9392bd6e14b2f34e...), lower-cased, duplicates left out, and thirteen entries left out
  that hold a string this repository keeps out of its files (see
  tools/read_hyphen_probe.py, FORBIDDEN).  Of the open English pattern sets measured, this
  one agrees with Word's breaks most often (ROADMAP.md, 3.9.1); it breaks the words
  en-moby.txt.gz does not hold.

  hyphen.tex's notice: "The Plain TeX hyphenation tables [NOT TO BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY!]
  Unlimited copying and redistribution of this file are permitted as long as this file
  is not modified. Modifications are permitted, but only if the resulting file is not
  named hyphen.tex."  No file here is named hyphen.tex.

  ushyphex.tex's notice: "Copyright 2021 TeX Users Group.  You may freely use, modify
  and/or distribute this file."


hyph-en-us.pat.txt, hyph-en-us.hyp.txt -- American English (hyph-utf8; kept for the probe)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The library's English patterns until the measurement of 3.9.1; tools/make_hyphen_probe.py
  still hyphenates its paragraphs "hyphenated by hand" with them, so that they stay the
  documents Word was shown.


  title: Hyphenation patterns for American English
  copyright: Copyright (C) 1990, 2004, 2005 Gerard D.C. Kuiken
  version: 2005-05-30 (ushyphmax.tex: Kuiken's patterns with Knuth's hyphen.tex)
  licence:
      Copying and distribution of this file, with or without modification,
      are permitted in any medium without royalty provided the copyright
      notice and this notice are preserved.

  The patterns include those of the Plain TeX hyphenation table (Donald E. Knuth,
  hyphen.tex), whose notice reads: "Unlimited copying and redistribution of this file
  are permitted as long as this file is not modified. Modifications are permitted, but
  only if the resulting file is not named hyphen.tex."  No file here is named hyphen.tex.

Considered and not used
-----------------------

* LibreOffice's hyph_de_DE.dic: LGPL 2 or later -- not compatible; not used.
* LibreOffice's hyph_nl_NL.dic (OpenTaal, BSD-3-Clause or CC BY 3.0): compatible, but on
  the probe's words it agrees with Word exactly as often as hyph-nl does, and it has no
  rules for spelling changes at a break; not used.
* hyph-en-gb (MIT): Word's en-GB breaks the probe's words as its en-US does, and of
  every English set measured it agrees with Word least; not used.
* LibreOffice's hyph_en_US.dic (hyphen.tex and the 2007 TUGboat log as patterns,
  BSD-style) and hyph_en_GB.dic: agree with Word a little less than hyph-en-us-knuth
  and no better than hyph-en-gb; not used.
* Patterns trained with pypatgen on the Moby Hyphenator list: 3 MB, and they agree with
  Word less often than the list itself or Knuth's patterns; not used (the list is used).
* LibreOffice's hyph_fr.dic (based on hyph-fr, LGPL 2.1 or later): not compatible; not used.
